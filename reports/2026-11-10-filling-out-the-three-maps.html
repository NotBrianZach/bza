Three things were named and they are three different artifacts with costs that differ by roughly two orders of magnitude. Two of them are an afternoon of writing. The third is bounded by the verifier rather than by how much anybody wants to type, and it carries a decision that cannot be made by whoever happens to be authoring.

Where the three maps actually stand

CountOf which practisable
Concept map (AREAS, mapped nodes)257 nodes in 21 areas0
Practice map (authored ontologies)34 concepts in 6 subjects34

291 concepts total, 34 of them teachable. The two numbers are not a coincidence of counting: every teachable concept lives in an authored ontology and every one of the 21 atlas areas is 0/N. The atlas is a map of a subject; the practice engine is a syllabus of what has been written up; and right now they do not overlap at all.

That is the honest shape of the thing, and it is why "fill out the map" splits into three jobs rather than one.

Map 1 — the concept map. Cheap, and it gates nothing.

A mapped node is five fields:

{
  id: 'fraction-arithmetic',
  match: /^fractions?\b|\b(common fraction|rational number)s?\b/i,
  name: 'Fraction arithmetic',
  is: 'Rational numbers as parts of a whole, with the four operations and the
       common-denominator step that drives them.',
  needs: ['gcd-and-lcm', 'negative-numbers'],
  terms: 'fraction numerator denominator common denominator',
}

No knowledge points, so isTeachable is false, so gatingPrerequisitesOf looks straight through it and it gates nothing. That is what makes this the safe map to extend: a wrong node is a wrong label on a diagram, not a wall in front of a learner.

The guards are already in place and will catch the realistic mistakes — atlasProblems rejects a dangling needs, a node colliding with an authored concept, and a cycle; tests/atlas.mjs additionally asserts no title matches more than four concepts, which is the failure mode of the match regexes.

Verdict: extend freely. The thinnest areas against what a reader actually arrives with are coordinate-geometry (7 nodes) and limits-and-continuity (9). Neither is a bottleneck for anything, which is also the point — this map is for orientation, and orientation is what it already does well.

Map 2 — the practice map. The verifier is the boundary.

This is the one that matters and the one that cannot be bulk-authored. Making a concept practisable is two pieces of work, and the second is the hard one:

  1. a Concept with knowledgePoints, each carrying a statement and a workedExample of {problem, subgoals[], answer};
  2. entries in TASKS in tasks.ts, each naming a knowledgePointId, a kind, a given, a form and a reference answer.

ontology-functions.ts states the constraint on (2) in its own header and it is the governing rule for everything proposed here:

It stops at graphs-of-functions deliberately, and the boundary is the verifier rather than the curriculum. […] ruleEvaluate settles exact rational arithmetic; it cannot rule "the graph is increasing here", and a task that asked it and then graded by keyword would be the proxy failure tasks.ts warns about dressed as a feature.

So the question for any candidate concept is not "is this worth teaching" but "can an answer to it be settled exactly". Of the five task kinds, three can:

transform_to_formyesthe CAS worker (equivalence)

The evaluate / transform_to_form split is a live operational concern, not a technicality. tasks.ts already records what happened the last time it was ignored: rational-expressions was authored almost entirely as transform_to_form, factoring-quadratics and polynomial-division are gated solely behind polynomial-arithmetic, and so whenever the maths worker is unreachable a learner who picked that subject meets a wall at its first concept with nothing behind it reachable by doing problems.

Any new subject must carry enough evaluate tasks to be walkable with the worker down. That is a constraint on authoring, not a nice-to-have.

The hazard in authoring over a mapped node

Both candidates below already exist as mapped nodes, and atlasProblems rejects an authored concept colliding with one. So authoring means moving the node, and atlas-structures.ts records what that costs in a comment written after it went wrong:

Removing a node removes every edge declared on its OWN needs, and that is the whole reason this block exists. It was learned the hard way one subject ago: authoring function-concept silently deleted variables-and-expressions → function-concept and made that syllabus a root of the entire map, practisable from a cold account by somebody who had never met a letter standing for a number.

The procedure is therefore fixed, and it is three steps rather than one:

  1. delete the mapped node from its area;
  2. add the authored concept, with knowledge points and tasks;
  3. restate every incoming edge — each needs entry on other nodes that named the departing node — in the area's links.

Step 3 is the one that gets forgotten, and the symptom is not an error. It is a syllabus quietly becoming a root of the map.

For fraction-arithmetic specifically there are five incoming edges to restate, and one of them changes the whole analysis — see the next section. The five: combining-rational, decimal-representation, ratio-and-rate, roots-and-irrationals, sample-spaces.

Map 3 — the narratives map. Cheap, but bounded by Map 2.

A journey is a destination plus prose; the steps are derived (claimableChainFor ordered by teachingOrder). Authoring one is twenty lines and no new mechanism.

The bound is that a journey must end on a teachable concept — journeyProblems rejects anything else, because a destination with no knowledge points is a goal nobody can reach. So the narratives map cannot outgrow the practice map, and there are 34 teachable concepts of which 6 are currently used as destinations.

That leaves real headroom without touching anything else. Unused destinations that carry a genuine arc:

DestinationTeachable chainThe arc

rational-exponents6why x^(1/2) has to mean a root if the rules are to survive

simplifying-rational8cancelling, and the domain you lose when you do

Verdict: extend freely, up to six more. This is the best value per hour of the three and needs no decision from anybody.

The decision this document cannot make

Both practice-map candidates sit beneath material that is already authored, and that direction is not symmetric with authoring above it.

ontology-functions.ts was authored above the algebra and could assert the safe version of the consequence: it created ~170 new gates, but "NO CONCEPT ANYBODY CAN PRACTISE GAINED A GATE" — everything that acquired one was mapped-only with no task to serve, so no learner's session changed.

Authoring beneath does the opposite. ontology-arithmetic.ts is the precedent: it tightened the gate, the entry point moved down, and polynomial-arithmetic stopped being introducible on a cold account.

And for fraction-arithmetic the usual middle course is not available, which is the single most important finding in this document.

combining-rational — a teachable concept in rational-expressions, and a step on the existing "Break a hard fraction into easy ones" journey — already declares needs: ['fraction-arithmetic', …]. The edge is in the atlas today. It does nothing only because fraction-arithmetic is mapped-only, so gatingPrerequisitesOf looks straight through it to whatever is teachable beyond.

The instant that concept acquires a single knowledge point, it gates combining-rational. No new edge, no decision point, no opt-out. The author does not get to add the material now and the gate later, because the gate is already written and is being held open solely by the absence of the material.

So the sequencing I would have recommended — concept first, edge second as its own reversible act — is not on the table here. The options are:

  1. Author it and accept the gate. Anyone part-way through rational expressions finds combining-rational gated behind fractions they have not practised. Pedagogically right; a real interruption for anyone mid-subject.
  2. Author it and remove combining-rational's needs entry first, so the gate arrives as a separate later change. This is the sequencing above, recovered — but it means deleting a true prerequisite edge for the duration, and an atlas that briefly asserts something false about the subject is a worse artifact than a gate that briefly inconveniences somebody.
  3. Leave it mapped. Which is the status quo and is at least honest: the route is drawn, nothing is written, and every surface says so.

I would take (1), because the gate is correct and the atlas should not be made to lie to soften a transition. But it is a decision with a visible cost for current learners, and it is not an authoring detail to be absorbed into a content commit.

linear-equations should be checked for the same property before anyone starts on it. It is in a different position — nothing teachable declares a need on it — so there the concept-then-edge sequencing probably is available.

The two gaps worth closing first

Chosen because each is a hole a reader would notice, and each is exactly rulable rather than merely desirable.

fraction-arithmetic (currently mapped in number-and-arithmetic). arithmetic-foundations has place value, whole-number arithmetic, order of operations, negative numbers and divisibility — and no fractions at all, while rational-expressions sits above it. Exactly rulable: exact rational arithmetic is precisely what exactValue does, so this can be authored mostly as evaluate tasks and is therefore walkable with the worker down — which also partly answers the all-transform_to_form problem recorded above. Carries the unavoidable gate described above.

In flight as of writing. The edit workspace edit-workspaces/bza/agent-librarian-scope contains an unfinished authoring of this concept — ontology-arithmetic.ts, tasks.ts and atlas-foundations.ts all modified, 35 teachable instead of 34 — which currently fails two pre-existing atlas invariants: "and they carry their own subject, not the area they left" and "combining rational expressions reaches arithmetic through the fractions link". The first is step 3 of the move procedure above (the authored concept must carry subjectId: 'arithmetic-foundations', not the area it departed). The second is the gate this document is about, arriving as predicted. None of it is committed and none of it is in the database, so the published atlas and production are unaffected — the checkout measures 291/34 with 163/163 passing. Whoever picks this up should treat the workspace copy as a draft to finish, not as a regression to revert.

linear-equations (currently mapped in algebra-foundations). You can reach the quadratic formula without ever having solved ax + b = c. Needs a check I have not done: whether a "solve for x" answer has a NamedForm the verifier can check, or whether it has to be posed as an evaluate on the root. That check gates the estimate — if there is no form for a solution, this concept is a larger job than it looks.

What I would not do

  • Bulk-author mapped nodes to make the atlas look fuller. 257 nodes already outrun the 34 practisable ones by 7:1, and every honest surface in the app has to say so — atlasCoverage prints it, the Concepts tab prints it, and each journey row prints how much of its own route is unwritten. Widening that ratio makes every one of those sentences worse.
  • Author a concept whose tasks cannot be settled exactly. The place that tempts is reading a graph, and ontology-functions.ts already declined it for the right reason.
  • Add the gating edges in the same change as the concepts. See above. Two acts, two decisions, and the second one is reversible on its own.
